1. Read the move before you read the pick

Steam

A fast, same-direction move across several books in a short window, usually from large or respected bets. Example: -2.5 to -3.5 at several books within minutes.

Reverse line movement (RLM)

The line moves against the side getting most public tickets. Most tickets on the favorite but -7 drops to -6: books may be reacting to money on the underdog.

The 5-question check, every move

  1. From where? Compare to the game-week opener, not last night's number.
  2. Through a key number? In the NFL, 3 and 7 are the most common final margins, then 10, 6, 4 and 14. A move from 2.5 to 3.5 matters far more than 4.5 to 5.5.
  3. Is there news? Injuries, a QB change and wind explain most "steam" that isn't steam.
  4. Did the price move too? -3 -110 to -3 -125 is a move even if the spread didn't change.
  5. Is the number still there? If the best price is gone, set a target and wait. Don't chase a stale number.

3. Hold and no-vig (fair) odds

Hold = 1 − 1 ÷ (both sides' implied probabilities added up): the book's expected cut if it takes balanced action, same as the free hold calculator. Fair odds strip the vig out so you see what the market really thinks.

Two-way marketHoldFair price, side 1

Shop the line. At -110 / -110 the hold is 4.55%; a book dealing -105 / -105 holds 2.38%, about half.

7. When NOT to bet a move

  • The move is all news (QB out, wind) and the new number is fair.
  • You're getting the worst number of the week after the move.
  • It's off a key number (3.5 to 2.5) and you'd be laying the bad side of it.
  • You'd be betting more than your normal unit to "catch up."
