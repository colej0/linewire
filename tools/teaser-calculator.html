Teasers and the NFL key numbers 3 and 7

A teaser moves every leg of a parlay by the same number of points in your favor, and you get a smaller payout in return. In the NFL that trade is only worth it when the extra points carry the line through 3 and 7, the two most common final margins. Moving a line from −8.5 to −2.5, or from +1.5 to +7.5, picks up both. Moving −3 to +3 or +10 to +16 crosses fewer common margins, so you pay the teaser price for less. This calculator flags which key numbers each leg gains: 3 means a loss now wins, and ½3 means the leg gains half a key number (a loss becomes a push, or a push becomes a win).

The price matters as much as the legs. Every extra half-point costs you payout, and each added team raises the hit rate every leg needs. Compare that per-leg breakeven with how often you think each leg covers. FAQ

What is a 6-point teaser in football?

A 6-point teaser is a parlay of two or more football bets where every spread or total moves 6 points in your favor: a −7.5 favorite becomes −1.5 and a +2.5 underdog becomes +8.5. Every leg must still win, and the payout is lower than a normal parlay.

What are the key numbers for NFL teasers?

3 and 7 are the most common NFL final margins, so teasers are strongest when each leg moves through both. With 6 points that means favorites of −7.5 to −8.5 teased down to −1.5 to −2.5, and underdogs of +1.5 to +2.5 teased up to +7.5 to +8.5.

How do you calculate teaser breakeven?

Convert the teaser price to decimal odds and take 1 ÷ decimal for the chance the whole ticket must win. For the chance each leg must cover, take that number to the power of 1 ÷ legs. A 2-team teaser at −120 (1.833) must win 54.5% of the time, so each leg needs about 73.9%.
