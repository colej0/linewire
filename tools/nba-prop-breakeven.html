Why breakeven matters for NBA player props

Every player prop has a hit rate you need just to break even, and it is set by the price. A points, rebounds, assists or PRA over at −110 needs 52.4%; at −130 it needs 56.5%. On pick'em apps the same idea applies per pick: a 3-pick Power Play at 6x needs every pick to hit 55.0%, so each pick is priced like a −122 bet. If your read on a player can't clear that number, the price is the problem, not the pick.

When you have both sides of a prop, the calculator strips out the vig to show the no-vig (fair) probability, which is the market's own estimate. Compare your hit rate with both numbers. FAQ

What hit rate do NBA props need to break even?

It depends on the price. Breakeven = 1 ÷ decimal odds: −110 needs 52.4%, −115 needs 53.5%, −120 needs 54.5% and −130 needs 56.5%. Plus-money props need less: +100 needs 50%.

How do you calculate breakeven for a pick'em slip?

For a slip where every pick must hit, per-pick breakeven = (1 ÷ multiplier) to the power of 1 ÷ picks. A 2-pick at 3x needs 57.7% per pick, a 3-pick at 6x needs 55.0%, and a 6-pick at 37.5x needs 54.7%.

What is the no-vig probability of a prop?

Convert both sides' odds to implied probabilities, then divide your side's probability by their sum. For −115/−105 that is 53.5% ÷ (53.5% + 51.2%) ≈ 51.1%. The gap between the sum and 100% is the book's hold.
